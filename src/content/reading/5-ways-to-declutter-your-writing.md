# 5 Ways to Declutter Your Writing

![rw-book-cover](https://s0.wp.com/i/blank.jpg)

## Metadata
- Author: [[The Thesis Whisperer]]
- Full Title: 5 Ways to Declutter Your Writing
- Category: #articles
- Summary:: In this article, the author shares 5 ways to declutter your writing based on ideas from William Zinsser's book "On Writing Well." The first tip is to eliminate "fuzz," or excess words, from your writing to improve clarity. The second tip is to pay attention to adverbs and remove unnecessary qualifiers. The third tip is to strive for nuance in your writing by carefully choosing words to convey your ideas. The fourth tip is to prune excess text and focus on simple and precise writing. Finally, the fifth tip is to embrace simplicity and clarity in your writing, which can demonstrate confidence and command of your material.

## Highlights
- All writers (will have to) edit their prose, but (the) great writers edit (it) viciously, always trying to eliminate (words which are) ‘fuzz’ – (excess) words (which are not adding anything of value). Zinsser compares (the process of editing out) ‘fuzz’ to fighting weeds – you will always be slightly behind (because they creep in when you aren’t looking for them). One of my (pet hates) is (the word) ‘also’. (If you search and replace all instances (of this word) you will find you can live without it and your writing will improve ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqdbgsdzyem717dsw13f1emj))
- There’s quite a difference between ‘argues’ and ‘asserts’. The first implies that Inger is making a case, the second implies that Inger is defending a position without necessarily providing any evidence for it. ‘Asserts’ adds a whiff of arrogance, but without over playing it (remember that academia is [in a state of **polite** warfare](http://thesiswhisperer.com/2010/11/01/the-stegosaurus-strategy/)). Likewise ‘stating’ something is different from ‘outlining’ it – the latter implies that some explanation is supplied which will help the reader understand what is being discussed. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqdbns6nsa8j29ktkeqb5pn0))
- Paying attention to the words you use to describe the work of others saves you the trouble of adding another sentence to explain to the reader what *you* think of the work. It’s the thesis writer’s equivalent of a nod and a wink to the reader. It’s hard work to remember all that nuance, so I keep a [handy list of verbs](http://www.teachers.ash.org.au/researchskills/dalton.htm) on my wall. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hqdc00de4cbrbc4pa0c2td2a))
- It can be tempting to ‘dress up’ your writing to appear more intelligent. Resist the urge. The ideas and findings in a thesis are important; style is secondary. A simple and precise style is like painting your walls white – a backdrop against which your ideas can pop. It can be hard to do the necessary pruning, but remember that examiners are likely to view a thinner thesis as a sign that you are confident and in charge of your material.
