- Category: #articles
- Summary:: The article discusses the phenomenon of forgetting most of what we learn and offers various perspectives on how to make sense of it. While knowledge fades fast, feelings or vibes tend to stick around longer. Education mainly focuses on imparting knowledge and skills rather than vibes, which are more powerful and important. Instead of drowning the brain in information, one needs to cultivate curiosity, perseverance, hope, and perspective, which are vibes. The article ends with two examples of classes, one with bad vibes and the other with good vibes.

## Highlights
- Learning is lossy and there’s no way around that. You have to encode 100 units of information if you want to retain even a few. This is simply a law of nature, and if you’re mad about it, wait until you hear about perpetual motion machines. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hyw49aew9xtv35yxt1fts2wt))
- The important stuff sticks. People remember multiplication because they keep using it. The fact that we lose so much means that we’re learning a bunch of useless stuff that our brains dutifully erase ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hyw4asekbj2z7v26db1tc69c))
    - Note: I know that you’re going to lose most of what I teach you then perhaps we are going about teaching and notetaking and lecturing all wrong
- You’ll often hear this from presidents of liberal arts colleges, as if you only start “learning how to learn” when you turn 18 and start paying $60,000 a year for the privilege. Shouldn’t learning how to learn be the *first* thing we do? What were we spending all of our time on? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hyx3c5q6kjmx20k2f4vmh2p4))
- We do, in fact, know quite a bit about how people learn, and almost nobody learns it. When you want to remember something you’ve read, do you highlight it, reread it, or summarize it afterward? Those strategies [don’t work very well](https://journals.sagepub.com/stoken/rbtfl/Z10jaVH/60XQM/full). Do you cram for tests? [Also bad.](https://sci-hub.se/10.1037/0021-9010.84.5.795) Plus, the “final” in “final exams” gives students license to purge their knowledge once the semester is over. Anybody who has “learned how to learn” shouldn’t do these things, but visit a university library at 2 AM during exam week and see how well that’s working out. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hyx3d5pxxqa7nb8457y802tt))
- None of this will happen, though, without proper vibe management. It is possible for teachers to send a vibe of “success in school depends on satisfying my whims.” Peers can give you the vibe of “this is all just a game before we go do whatever will pay us the most.” Buildings can say “it’s cool to cause the opioid crisis as long as you [donate some money afterward](https://www.bostonglobe.com/metro/2019/12/05/list-what-carries-sackler-name-and-what-doesn-anymore/lfiNDA9jKB3OdyO7isKAkL/story.html).” Nobody ever has to state any of this explicitly, and usually nobody does. Vibes are like dark energy: invisible, but evident everywhere. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01hyyss7nvm34xvpcr0d2fktm8))
