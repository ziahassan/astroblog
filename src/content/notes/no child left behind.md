Five bullet points on No Child Left Behind:

#edpolicy 

1. The No Child Left Behind Act (NCLB), passed in 2001, was a U.S. Act of Congress that reauthorized the Elementary and Secondary Education Act and included various measures designed to improve the performance of U.S. primary and secondary schools by increasing accountability.

2. The act required states to develop assessments in basic skills, which had to be administered to all students in certain grades in order to receive federal funding for schools. This was intended to ensure that all students received a fair, equal, and significant opportunity to obtain a high-quality education.

3. NCLB was based on the belief that setting high standards and establishing measurable goals could improve individual outcomes in education. It also aimed at closing the achievement gap between students by ensuring that poor and minority children were not left behind.

4. The Act has been criticized for being too focused on tests and scores, leading many teachers to "teach to the test" instead of providing a comprehensive education. Critics argue that this approach has led to neglecting other areas of a child's educational development.

5. In 2015, the NCLB Act was replaced by the[[ Every Student Succeeds Act]] (ESSA), which revised some of the testing requirements and allowed greater flexibility for states in setting their own standards.
