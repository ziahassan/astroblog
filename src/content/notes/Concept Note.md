A **concept note** is a brief, concise document that outlines the **core idea of a proposed research project**, typically used in the early stages of grant funding applications. It helps funders quickly assess whether a project aligns with their priorities before requesting a full proposal.

---

### **🔍 Purpose of a Concept Note**

- **Gauge interest** from funders before investing time in a full proposal.
    
- **Summarize the problem, solution, and significance** in a compelling way.
    
- **Demonstrate feasibility** and researcher expertise.
    

---

### **📄 Typical Structure of a Concept Note (1–3 pages)**

  

Here’s what it usually includes:

1. **Title**
    
    A clear and descriptive title of your project.
    
2. **Background / Rationale**
    
    What problem are you addressing and why is it important?
    
3. **Objectives / Research Questions**
    
    What are the main goals or hypotheses?
    
4. **Methodology / Approach**
    
    How will you conduct the research? What methods will you use?
    
5. **Expected Outcomes**
    
    What results do you anticipate and how will they make an impact?
    
6. **Significance / Alignment**
    
    Why is this relevant to the funder’s mission or strategic goals?
    
7. **Timeline and Budget Summary** _(optional)_
    
    A rough sense of the project duration and scale of funding needed.
    
8. **Principal Investigator(s) Info**
    
    Brief bios or qualifications of the lead researchers.
    

---

### **✍️ Tips for Writing a Strong Concept Note**

- Keep it **clear, concise, and jargon-free**.
    
- Tailor it to the **specific funder’s goals or priorities**.
    
- Emphasize **novelty, feasibility, and potential impact**.
    
- Be specific enough to show rigor, but short enough to stay skimmable.
