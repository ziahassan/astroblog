Pedagogy of poverty means that the students condition the teachers in urban environments; our policies and ways of being are centered around the fact that these are students experiencing poverty.


The term "pedagogy of poverty" refers to a traditional teaching model often used in schools serving low-income communities. This model tends to focus on basic skills and rote memorization, rather than critical thinking or problem-solving. It is often criticized for its limitations and for reinforcing negative stereotypes about students living in poverty. The idea is that this pedagogy perpetuates a cycle of poverty by not providing students with the skills needed to break out of their socio-economic status.

The term also implies that our educational policies and practices are influenced by the assumption that students in poverty are less capable or have fewer opportunities, which can lead to lower expectations and fewer resources for these students.

In many ways, it reflects an educational system that is more about managing poverty than about genuinely educating students, adapting teaching methods, or addressing the root causes of poverty. It's about maintaining the status quo rather than challenging it.

Critics argue that instead of a pedagogy of poverty, we need a [[pedagogy of possibility]]—one that recognizes the potential in all students, regardless of their socio-economic status. This would mean providing high-quality education that fosters critical thinking, creativity, and problem-solving skills. It would also mean addressing systemic issues like underfunded schools and income inequality.
