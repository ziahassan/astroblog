TAM stands for **Technology Acceptance Model**, a widely used theoretical framework in information systems research that explains how users come to accept and use technology.

## Key Components of TAM:

**Core Beliefs:**
- **Perceived Usefulness (PU)** - The degree to which a person believes using a technology will enhance their job performance
- **Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU)** - The degree to which a person believes using a technology will be free of effort

**The Model Flow:**
External Variables → Perceived Usefulness & Perceived Ease of Use → Attitude Toward Using → Behavioral Intention → Actual System Use

## Key Principles:
- Perceived ease of use influences perceived usefulness
- Both factors influence attitude toward using the technology
- Attitude affects behavioral intention to use
- Behavioral intention predicts actual usage

## Applications:
- Software adoption in organizations
- E-commerce acceptance
- Mobile app usage
- Educational technology implementation
- Healthcare information systems

## Evolution:
TAM has evolved into TAM2 and TAM3, incorporating additional factors like social influence, job relevance, and computer anxiety.

The model is valuable for predicting technology adoption and helping organizations understand why users may accept or reject new technologies.
