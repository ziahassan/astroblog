> The Anna persona ceremony is a popular Korean tradition also known as Doljanchi. This celebration marks the first birthday of a baby and holds a significant place in Korean culture. During the ceremony, the child is placed in front of a table set with various objects such as rice, string, money, pencils, or a knife.
> 
> Each item symbolizes a different career or future for the child. For instance, if the child reaches for rice or food, it signifies that they will not be hungry in their life. If they reach for money, it suggests prosperity and wealth. If they choose a pencil or book, it means that they will have an academic or scholarly life.
> 
> Choosing a knife generally indicates that the child will become skillful with their hands and could predict professions like being a chef, surgeon or artisan.
> 
> Remember this tradition may vary from family to family and some may opt for different objects based on personal beliefs and family customs. It's more of an entertaining prediction rather than something serious to base one's future on.
