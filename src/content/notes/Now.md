This month, I got to take a class with my hero [[Brian Eno]]. I've learned a lot so far, such as [[older people write flatter melodies]], and [[a good melody comes out immediately]]. 

Learning about how Herbie Hancock writes a melody, we come to realize that [[funk is about what you leave out]].

It's important to create characters when you're making; truth telling is boring since [[art is about fiction even when it's attempting to tell the truth]].

I've noticed it before but never quite fixated on it... [[recipes are poetry]].
To see more things like this, learn to see like an artist: [[to see something like an artist would, deconstruct the concept in your mind]].

I guess I've been thinking a lot like an artist, figuring that [[you can either be motivated by curiosity or by shame and guilt]].

Listening has become massively important as a result of this Eno class. Like how [[luck is about being ready for possibility]]. 

---

Since it's been holiday season, I've been thinking a lot about gift giving. I learned that [[the best gift you can give a recipient of your writing is being as concise as possible]]. Also that [[gift-giving is relational, not transactional]].

An important lesson this month is: [[never interrupt someone who is having a hard time explaining because that's their thinking process]].

Also, something that's been on my mind recently is Linear Models, a subject that I'm currently studying in my classes at Johns Hopkins. The textbook is all about econometrics, and the idea that [[all economic theory is based on the idea that we want to maximize our utility]]. All of the models of supply demand are based on this (reasonable) notion.
