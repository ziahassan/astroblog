> [!Abstract]
>
> This article introduces the concept of
              effectivity,
              which is the power to be effective or to achieve a certain effect to accomplish one’s goals, in this case, desired school or life achievement. Giftedness would be better defined and developed in terms of effectivity than in terms of various concepts of ability. The concept of effectivity hinges on the idea that what determines, or what influences achievement, includes cognitive ability, but only as a small part of the total picture. Gifted “underachievement” as well as “overachievement” disappear when one understands giftedness in terms of effectivity.

> These factors, taken together, influence what we here call effectivity, which is defined as the power to be effective or achieve a certain effect (www.vocabulary.com), in this case, desired school or life achievement. The concept of effectivity is based on the notion that what determines, or at least influences, achievement includes cognitive ability, but only as a small part of the metaphorical equation. To refer to someone as under- or over-achieving based on a comparison between a score on a limited (ability) test and a score on another limited (achievement) test, or with school grades, presents a narrow view of the full range of knowledge, skills, attitudes, and other characteristics that underlie achievement, in school or anyplace else. What matters is not merely ability but also effectivity—one’s overall potential to achieve effectively at particular desired levels of achievement. If one adopts effectivity as a notion, the notions of under- and overachievement go away. What are the elements of effectivity?
