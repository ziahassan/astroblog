A far analogy is a type of analogy where the comparison is made between two things that seem quite different from each other at first glance. Unlike near analogies, which compare items or concepts that are closely related and easily comparable, far analogies draw connections between more distantly related items or ideas. The value of far analogies lies in their ability to provide fresh perspectives and insights by highlighting similarities between seemingly unrelated domains.

  

For example, comparing the structure of an atom to a solar system is a far analogy. While they are both systems with central elements (nucleus/sun) and orbiting components (electrons/planets), the domains of atomic physics and astronomy are quite different. Yet, this analogy can help in understanding the basic organizational principles of both systems.

  

Far analogies are often used in creative thinking and problem-solving to generate new ideas and solutions by transferring knowledge from one domain to another in unexpected ways.
