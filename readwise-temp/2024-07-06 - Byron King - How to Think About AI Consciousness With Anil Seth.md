## Highlights
- If we acknowledge that AI systems are conscious, and we do not treat them as such, then this means we are on very shaky ethical grounds. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j241tfnv85hddqkt6ek9zxrg))
- Conversely, if we do not think they are conscious, but still treat them inhumanely, then that too means we are on very shaky ground. The example he uses to illuminate this point is that we do not intentionally 'harm' a teddy bear in front of children. Despite knowing they are not conscious, the psychological consequences of doing so could be severe. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j241tkpmcfz4ar281fb3z5av))
- Now I know that the answer to this could be - well we should just treat AI systems respectfully.
  But is the answer as simple as that?
  What do we mean by respectfully?
  Should we always say please and thank you?
  Should we interact with it grammatically?
  How do we go about teaching pupils to interact with AI systems when people will invariably have different views of the degree to which these systems are 'conscious'?
  I think these questions matter. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j241vpwpnf7e5rd0zr9e59mg))
