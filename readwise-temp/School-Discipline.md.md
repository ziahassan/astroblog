# School Discipline

![rw-book-cover](https://readwise-assets.s3.amazonaws.com/static/images/article0.00998d930354.png)

## Metadata
- Author: [[American Federation of Teachers]]
- Full Title: School Discipline
- Category: #articles
- Document Tags: [[ed policy]] 
- Summary:: Many students, especially African-American and Latino youth, face unfair school discipline practices that lead to suspensions and negative outcomes. These suspensions disrupt learning and increase the risk of dropping out. The American Federation of Teachers suggests reforms to create fairer discipline policies and provide better support for all students.
- URL: https://www.aft.org/position/school-discipline

## Highlights
- Once a youth has had contact with law enforcement or has been placed into a juvenile justice facility, the stigma cannot be erased; this sets the trajectory for ongoing negative and disruptive contact with school personnel, law enforcement, peers and families. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jaas8ngxp03jy0shd8969c06))
- research has shown that even a single suspension increases the likelihood of low achievement and of dropping out of school altogether. African-American and Latino males are far more likely to receive more-punitive consequences for school behavior infractions. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jaas96w108c9n7dzrx2qvjdg))
- too frequently, these punishments are for nonviolent, noncriminal behavior that could have been addressed or remedied within the school community. Yet, research shows that African-American and Latino students, particularly males, are more likely to be suspended for subjective violations, like disrespect, insubordination or willful defiance, that require interpretation and often receive disparate administration of consequences. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jaas9rctzgv7gcjp2a19v3wc))
