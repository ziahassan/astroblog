## Highlights
- Countries that lead in AI education will subsequently lead in AI-driven economic growth and military advancement. If the U.S. doesn’t prioritize AI literacy and readiness, it isn’t just setting students up for failure — it’s undermining its own economic and national security. By 2030, artificial intelligence will contribute nearly [$20 trillion to the global economy](https://www.idc.com/getdoc.jsp?containerId=prUS52600524). America must stay at the forefront. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jvj0q9btjp9majf8ypfm2jmk))
- This means AI literacy — the fundamental understanding of these technological tools — isn’t optional. Neither is AI readiness, the ability to leverage those tools to the nation’s advantage. Instilling both concepts in America’s schools will set the foundation for the future. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jvj0qkhfayz69509x524hrsz))
- There’s also lots of room for nonprofit organizations to pick up the slack on AI education. Organizations like [aiEDU](http://aiedu.org) (where I work), [AIandYou](https://aiandyou.org/) and MIT’s [RAISE](https://raise.mit.edu/) initiative have done a lot of legwork creating pilot programs and teacher training so schools don’t have to figure it out from scratch. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01jvj0s3q0h8fz3a6yvrbwdk7p))
