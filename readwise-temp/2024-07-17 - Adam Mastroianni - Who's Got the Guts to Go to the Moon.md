## Highlights
- Discovering that some kids *might* benefit from some drugs is, I guess, better than setting the research money on fire. But this ain’t a moonshot. It’s so far from a moonshot that it needs a different name. Earthshot? Groundshot? How about, “normal research”? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31eg7q1ga5qv3rh9dk0f8gz))
- Here’s a dead giveaway: one of the first things the NIH touts about its Moonshot is that [it has published over 2,000 papers](https://substack.com/redirect/d53d536b-f84a-41f0-9e64-e58a04f0506b?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c). I’ve been in this game long enough to know that when a scientist actually has something important to tell you, they tell you about what they *did*, not what they *published*. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31ej0crqdjxp58gpespv4vb))
- There’s a useful adage here: “[Institutions will try to preserve the problem to which they are the solution](https://substack.com/redirect/0d58ca11-af32-421b-a7eb-33fa1a8b3fa6?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c).” I don’t think anyone involved in the Cancer Moonshot literally wants cancer to exist forever. But they all seem to *assume* that it will, and that the process of curing it will go on indefinitely, and they should all be well-funded in the meantime. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31ejzx77czb9k92rpm25w8x))
- The real problem with science is not the lack of riskiness; it’s the lack of *[seriousness](https://substack.com/redirect/1355dec2-093e-4be8-b69b-1352c9cd3309?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c)*. The first question we should ask isn’t “How likely is this project to fail?” but “Does this person believe in truth and seek it sincerely? Are they going to produce papers, or are they going to produce knowledge?” If the prerequisite for getting on the grant review committee is “you’ve produced a lot of papers,” good luck with this one. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31eqzdpb7j96cz0317gv8r7))
- There is, however, a different kind of risk that nobody seems willing to take: *reputational* risk. As in, how much will people make fun of you for funding this idea, especially if it doesn’t work? ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31ern76y7apngfkpek052h0))
- Courage is picking the good ideas regardless of whether people are cheering or booing. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31esr5h4489kr5fpgna2n83))
- If you’ve got the cash, taste, and guts necessary to find and fund stupid-looking-but-good ideas, you have *zero* competitors. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31etd875tvv69qazdstjqdh))
- 1. Experts and authorities ignore or suppress it because it seems stupid. This can go on for a long time.
  2. Eventually the new idea wins out because it’s better.
  #2 is where the money is waiting to be made. If you’ve got a good eye for ideas and you’re brave enough to tiptoe a bit outside the mainstream consensus, you can make a breakthrough decades or centuries earlier than it would have happened otherwise. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31evyxv5erf7qqd3bx6v0m1))
- When a rural doctor named Heinrich Henziger was like, “Maybe it’s caused by a lack of iodine,” the medical authorities shouted him down. One leading expert published a piece in the main Swiss medical journal saying the iodine deficiency theory “must be vigorously opposed.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31exmcd1b2tyqt59j28pf5f))
- The thing that causes goiter is indeed a lack of iodine, but the thing that prevented goiter from being cured was a lack of *courage*. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31exrcxc2wf5z2gqz2mf2ps))
- So you can only call something a moonshot if what you really mean is, “we’re attempting to do something important that won’t be appreciated until years after we accomplish it.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31f7tcdeymsj1s7q7x61cjm))
- So here’s the test: whatever “high-risk high-reward” program you’re running, would it have funded Karikó before she got famous? Do you have the courage and insight it takes to see someone’s potential when they aren’t legibly successful yet? If so, hats off to you. If not, you’re not doing a moonshot. You’re just doing normal science in a futuristic font. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31f9e7q6423fka3p35ftrnj))
- Most scientists [say](https://substack.com/redirect/aa97f7d2-1242-4cbd-ac5f-7c9adb69214e?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c) they would spend their grant dollars differently if they could, and some of the most important discoveries have come from researchers who were allowed to [screw around](https://substack.com/redirect/1c1322ad-9457-4d3a-bcfb-1fa2405778db?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c). People have noticed this, and now [there’s](https://substack.com/redirect/12e9d561-9e29-4214-af1c-5f760175a18e?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c) [a](https://substack.com/redirect/6e3d64b6-5ba3-412e-869c-cfc9e4cda052?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c) [widespread](https://substack.com/redirect/d3dab029-43dd-4fd5-b096-e8266a9066e2?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c) [feeling](https://substack.com/redirect/133cb1eb-56d8-49bf-bfd6-a81595995f8e?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c) that we should “fund people, not projects.” ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31fba1h2jpw4v23jngpkbe4))
- I support de-booby-trapping academia, but a better route is to build new places that don’t have any trap doors or poison darts at all. Not an appendix to an existing institution, not a retirement home for scholars who have achieved mainstream success, not a place where academics can hang out for a while before they go back to running from giant boulders—a completely separate space that works on different principles and supports an entirely different species of scientist. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31fdjaasajbexncm73h503w))
- Instead of running another 500 piddling, confounded little studies, someone with guts, taste, and money could run the **actually good study**.
  For instance:
  • Does flossing work? “[The evidence is low to very low-certainty.](https://substack.com/redirect/3a9ed511-5c10-4734-b38a-3dfa44d7f3c4?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c)” You could randomly assign a big group of people to floss or not, or tell everybody to floss half of their teeth, and see whether dentists can tell the difference.
  • Does sunscreen prevent skin cancer? “[The certainty of the evidence was low](https://substack.com/redirect/6812435f-d416-4f7c-ab50-f19d6a683db9?j=eyJ1IjoiNDRwN3pxIn0.RRrMGulKJTRZ8_w_N_UmQYRl6O8gWe_pLDWjF0Rvf_c).” You could randomly assign a big group of people to wear different kinds of sunscreen, or none at all, and follow them for the decades that it would take for any differences to show up. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01j31ffa4bm7ygg3hwrm8hyzks))
